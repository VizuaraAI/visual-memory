## New, verified 11 August 2026

**Chen, Zhao, Liu, Bai, Lin, Zhou, Chang.** *An Image is Worth 1/2 Tokens After
Layer 2: Plug-and-Play Inference Acceleration for Large Vision-Language Models.*
arXiv:2403.06764. Accepted to ECCV 2024, oral.
Abstract states that "the attention computation over visual tokens is of extreme
inefficiency in the deep layers", and prunes visual tokens after an early layer,
reporting large speedups without performance loss.
**Role in our paper:** the clearest statement of the redundancy premise that
claim 5 tests on a hybrid.

**Yang, Chen, Tian, Wang, Li, Yu, Jia.** *VisionZip: Longer is Better but Not
Necessary in Vision Language Models.* arXiv:2412.04467.
Abstract states that "the visual tokens generated by popular vision encoders,
such as CLIP and SigLIP, contain significant redundancy".
**Role in our paper:** second, independent statement of the same premise, and
evidence it is a family of methods rather than one paper.

**Jelassi, Brandfonbrener, Kakade, Malach.** *Repeat After Me: Transformers are
Better than State Space Models at Copying.* arXiv:2402.01032.
Proves transformers can copy exponentially long strings while generalized state
space models are "fundamentally limited by their fixed-size latent state", and
shows pretrained transformers "dramatically outperform state space models at
copying and retrieving information from context".
**Role in our paper:** this is the prior-work anchor, and it is the single
greatest threat to our novelty claim. It must be cited prominently and
delineated against explicitly, not buried.

## What this forces in the writing

The novelty section cannot claim we discovered that recurrent models lose
information from context. Jelassi et al. established that, with a proof. Any
draft that implies otherwise will be rejected by a reviewer who knows the
literature, and rightly.

What remains genuinely ours, stated against that background:

1. **A quantitative, predictable horizon.** Jelassi et al. give a capacity
   argument about fixed-size state. We give a number of tokens, computed from
   the model's own A and dt with no fitting, and confirm it against a measured
   curve. Their result says the state must eventually fail; ours says when, and
   why that particular when.

2. **Content selectivity.** The failure is not uniform. At the same instant, and
   from the same state, object identity is highly decodable while count and
   position are near chance. That is a claim about what the compression keeps,
   which a copying-capacity result does not address.

3. **Causal localisation inside a hybrid.** The splice shows the answer follows
   the attention channel and not the recurrent one, in a model that has both.
   Jelassi et al. compare architectures; we intervene within one.

4. **A consequence for a live method family.** If the attention layers hold the
   only copy, the redundancy premise behind Chen et al. and Yang et al. does not
   transfer to hybrids. That is new and it is actionable.

## Tone requirement for claim 5

Chen et al. and Yang et al. developed and validated their methods on dense
transformers, where they work. The paper says the premise does not transfer to
hybrids and measures where it stops holding. It does not say those methods are
wrong. Written as a takedown, this section hands a hostile reviewer to us from
exactly the community most likely to be assigned the paper.
